# The Best Inhaler in Thailand 2026: What Locals Use and Why Tourists Buy It

If you have spent any time in Thailand, you have seen them. On the bus, at the night market, in the gym, at the office: someone pulls a small tube from their pocket, holds it below their nose, and takes a slow breath. No smoke, no fuss, just a quiet aromatic ritual that has been part of daily Thai life for generations.

That small tube is a yadom, the traditional Thai herbal inhaler. And if you are trying to figure out which one to buy, either while you are here or before you visit, this guide covers exactly that: what locals actually use, where to find them, and what your options are if you want to bring the tradition home.

## The Inhaler You Keep Seeing in Thailand

The yadom (ยาดม) is one of those objects so embedded in Thai daily life that most locals do not think of it as anything unusual. It is as ordinary as a pack of gum. Schoolchildren carry them. Office workers keep them in desk drawers. Taxi drivers have them on the dashboard. Muay Thai fighters use them at the gym.

The word itself tells you what it does: ya means herb, and dom means to inhale. A small tube packed with aromatic botanicals, typically built around menthol, camphor, and various herbal compounds, that delivers a cool, clarifying aromatic rush when you breathe through it.

For the full story of where this tradition comes from and why it has lasted so long, see our guide to [what yadom is and its history](/blog/what-is-yadom-thai-herbal-inhaler).

## Why Thais Carry an Inhaler Every Day

The yadom tradition grew out of Thailand's deep herbal heritage, drawing on centuries of botanical knowledge shared across Southeast Asia. What began as a folk preparation carried by healers and travelers gradually became a mainstream everyday object across the twentieth century.

Today it serves a simple purpose for most people: a fast sensory reset. Clear breathing on a hot, polluted day. A cooling hit of alertness when energy dips. A grounding aromatic moment in a chaotic environment. It is portable, instant, and costs almost nothing.

## The Most Popular Thai Inhaler Brands You Will Find in 7-Eleven

Walk into any 7-Eleven, Boots, or Watsons in Thailand and you will find at least one of these brands on the shelf. They are the everyday options, widely available and very affordable, typically priced between 30 and 40 Thai baht.

### Poy-Sian

Poy-Sian is the most recognized Thai inhaler brand globally. The green and white tube is iconic. It delivers a strong, immediate menthol hit built primarily around synthetic menthol and camphor. Consistent, effective for its core purpose, and available in virtually every convenience store in the country. The default choice for a first yadom experience at the lowest possible price.

### Hong Thai

Hong Thai is similarly priced and widely stocked across convenience stores and pharmacies in Thailand. It has a slightly warmer herbal base than Poy-Sian, though the formula remains synthetic-dominant. A reliable everyday option with strong local familiarity.

### Jarungjit

Jarungjit is the most traditionally oriented of the mainstream brands, using a broader range of botanicals and producing a more rounded, complex aroma than the larger mass-market names. It is respected within Thailand as a closer expression of the traditional yadom formula. Slightly harder to find than Poy-Sian, but worth seeking out in a dedicated pharmacy or herbal shop if you want something with more genuine herbal character at an accessible price.
